§3 · §6

Confidence the architecture ignores is confidence the system cannot act on.

Per-pixel confidence is not new in depth completion. What is new here is that it is consumed. The predicted reliability map R̂ is handed to the affinity network of a CSPN++ refiner, where it sets how strongly each pixel absorbs its neighbours across six propagation steps.

Nothing supervises R̂. There are no reliability labels and no reliability loss. Its only gradient arrives through the cost of gating badly: trusting a pixel that is wrong lets the error propagate outward, and the refinement loss rises. The one configuration that minimises the loss is the one where reliability anti-correlates with the true error of the coarse estimate.

w = 0.8 · softmax( AffNet([ u₁ ; R̂ ]) ) (Eq. 12)
D(t+1)(p) = Ms · d̃s + (1 − Ms) · Σi≠c wi D(t)(p + δi) (Eq. 13)
centre weight fixed at 0.2 · T = 6 iterations · anchors held as hard Dirichlet conditions

The panel opposite is the experiment that isolates the mechanism. One trained network, one coarse map D₀, one propagation block. Only the gate changes. A uniform gate matches ungated propagation, so the extra input channel is worth nothing by itself. A spatially shuffled gate preserves the value distribution, destroys the correspondence, and lands worse than not gating at all.

Δgen = RMSE(D₀) − RMSE(B) = 0.051 m — any refiner would get this
Δrel = RMSE(B) − RMSE(E) = 0.020 m — attributable to the gate
ηrel = Δrel / (Δgen + Δrel) ≈ 28% (Eqs. 25–27)

Stated in that direction deliberately: the larger share of the refinement gain is generic. Gating contributes the smaller 0.020 m — still the difference between matching CSPN++ and matching PENet at 67× fewer parameters, but a 28% share, not a majority.

Abstract

Three gaps, closed by four contributions.

Depth completion densifies sparse range measurements into metric depth. Reliable deployment needs more than accuracy: it needs an indication of where those predictions may be wrong. Existing methods optimise accuracy and rarely produce calibrated per-pixel reliability; where confidence does exist it is typically terminal — reported and then discarded — or applied once at a fixed fusion point.

R³DC integrates reliability directly into refinement, and RADI measures whether that reliability is ranked correctly, exploited, and numerically meaningful. A spatially shuffled control shows the improvement depends on the correct spatial allocation of reliability, not on the presence of an extra input channel.

Reliability as a control signal

R̂ feeds the affinity network of a reliability-gated CSPN++ refiner. Low-confidence pixels take in more neighbourhood context; high-confidence pixels resist modification; sparse measurements are enforced as hard Dirichlet boundary conditions. Learned with no reliability ground truth.

A measurable framework

RADI decomposes confidence quality into orthogonal, independently falsifiable quantities — REC, RBS, CAL, retaining AUSE — each reported globally and across edge, textureless and far-depth regions, with five baselines scored under one protocol.

A negative result with consequences

On synthesised ground truth REC inverts. We derive analytically that this is an artefact of the generator, not model overconfidence, which bounds the validity of any reliability evaluation on simulated depth — including our own aerial benchmarks.

Generality at 1.95–11.22 M

One recipe — identical losses, loss weights, optimizer and augmentation — across ground-level, aerial, UAV and indoor settings, with competitive KITTI accuracy at 6.5–67× fewer parameters and a 16,642-parameter head for the indoor case.

Of two models with identical RMSE, one may signal exactly where it is unreliable and the other may be uniformly and silently overconfident. Benchmarks rank them equally.

Scope of claims

We do not claim state-of-the-art raw depth accuracy on KITTI. BP-Net and GuideFormer reach lower RMSE. The claim concerns the accuracy–efficiency–reliability trade: competitive accuracy at substantially fewer parameters, plus an actionable per-pixel reliability signal that is explicitly evaluated and used to guide refinement.

What is not new here

Per-pixel confidence in depth completion (NCNN, pNCNN, branch confidence), spatial propagation, cross-modal attention, and ECE as a calibration measure. What is new is confidence used as a control signal for an iterative refiner's affinity weights, learned through the downstream objective; a three-part evaluation that separates ranking, exploitation and calibration; a controlled head-to-head of five reliability estimators; and evidence that synthetic ground truth can reverse their ranking.

§3 · Method

Reveal, then revise.

A single decoder cannot resolve the tension the task contains. Inferring global structure where no measurement exists wants large, smooth receptive fields; respecting sharp boundaries near anchors wants the opposite. Separating the two stages lets each use the mechanism it needs — and the reliability map is the interface between them.

How reliability is supervised without labels

R̂ receives no direct supervision. Its learning signal arrives along two indirect paths. The gating gradient: because R̂ enters Eq. 12, the loss on the refined prediction D₁ propagates through the affinity network and into the reliability head — assigning high reliability to an erroneous region permits inappropriate propagation and raises the SILog and Focal-BerHu terms. Shared-feature coupling: the Laplace NLL trains σ̂ from the same decoder features u₁ that the reliability head reads, giving a shared basis for estimating residual uncertainty and reliability.

This predicts where calibration should be strongest — near anchors and at boundaries, where propagation can change the output — and weakest in anchor-poor interiors. The region breakdown bears that out: far-depth, the region with the fewest returns, has the lowest REC.

Log-space normalisation

d̃ = [ ln(d + ε) − ln(dmin + ε) ] / [ ln(dmax + ε) − ln(dmin + ε) ]  ∈ [0,1]
ε = 10⁻³ · compresses an 8× range difference between the 80 m outdoor and 10 m indoor settings

This, together with the sparse-anchor term and SILog, is why one set of loss weights covers all four domains unchanged.

The composite objective

Five constraints — photometric accuracy, geometric consistency, structural fidelity, metric anchoring and uncertainty calibration — produce conflicting gradients when merged into a single term. Weights were fixed by grid search on a held-out KITTI split, then frozen across every dataset.

  • RevealDual-stream encoder, widths {B/2, B, 2B, 4B} at scales {1, ½, ¼, ⅛}. The depth stream's stride-2 layers use DCN v2 with zero-initialised offset convolutions, so each block is the identity at step 0 and learns to gather from valid measurements rather than averaging a mostly-empty grid.
  • FuseDepth features query RGB keys and values at three scales, with group normalisation on the query branch and logits clamped to [−8, 8] for FP16 stability. Attention is bounded to O(N²ₘₐₓ) by pooling to 512 tokens — a 384×640 input would otherwise produce 61,440 tokens at half resolution.
  • ReviseReliability-gated CSPN++ for six iterations. Neighbour weights sum to 0.8 so the centre retains a fixed 0.2; pixels never reached by propagation fall back to the coarse estimate. Learned anisotropic diffusion, in which predicted reliability sets the effective diffusivity.
  • IndoorFor NYU the encoder is replaced by a frozen Depth-Anything-V2 ViT-S and only the Indoor Calibration Head trains: a three-layer perceptron on pooled bottleneck features predicting a scale–shift pair, plus the output heads and affinity network. The gap between a linear and a non-linear head is the informative part — relative-to-metric depth is not a global affine transform, which is the assumption most scale-recovery pipelines make.
  • CostThe reliability and uncertainty heads read features the depth decoder has already computed; the affinity network gains one input channel. Together under 2% of the parameter budget, with no measurable change to the 50.4 ms single-pass latency.
§4 · RADI

Three questions a deployment engineer asks that RMSE cannot answer.

Consider a vehicle approaching a textureless wall. The model predicts 15 m; if it also reports low reliability, the planner can defer to radar or slow until parallax resolves the ambiguity, and the cost is a mild loss of efficiency. Now consider a specular puddle predicted at 10 m with reliability 0.85. The depth error may be identical. RMSE scores them identically. The second is the one that causes an accident.

Why three numbers rather than one

A model can rank its errors correctly and never act on the ranking — high REC, zero RBS. It can act on a ranking whose absolute values are meaningless — high RBS, poor CAL. It can be numerically calibrated on average while ranking nothing correctly inside a region — good CAL, near-zero REC. Collapsing them into a scalar hides exactly the failure modes that matter at deployment.

Sparsification is computed entirely on the output map, so AUSE is blind to whether the architecture consumed the ordering; and being invariant to rescaling, it is blind to whether a stated confidence of 0.8 means 80% of pixels are correct. A method can have excellent AUSE and still be useless on both counts. REC is preferred over the curve for three practical reasons: one number per region, a standard significance test, and a sign — so the inverted-confidence failure mode is visible rather than folded into an area.

Negative RBS is a real failure mode. It means the revise stage made the flagged pixels worse — a miscalibrated gate. RADI surfaces it; RMSE does not.

Region masks

All components are reported over four regions, computed on the ground truth and applied identically to every method: all (global baseline); edge, Sobel magnitude > 0.05, the discontinuities that dominate downstream failure; textureless, local luminance standard deviation < 8, where RGB guidance is weakest; and far-depth, d > 0.75 dmax, where sensor noise dominates.

RADI is model-agnostic. Any method producing a confidence map can be scored; for single-stage methods pass D₀ ≡ D₁ and report RBS as not applicable rather than as a zero score. If you use it, please state the configuration — Bcal = 15, τ = 0.10 — and the depth space in which RBS was computed, since log-space and metric-space values are not comparable.

§5.1 · Negative result

On synthetic ground truth, REC inverts — and it is the target's fault.

On VisDrone and Drone-Videos, REC is negative throughout training. Read naively, that is the worst failure a confidence-aware system can have: most certain exactly where it is most wrong. It is not what is happening, and the distinction matters beyond this paper.

The error measured against a synthesised target carries the generator's own deviation from reality:

εpred = |d̂ − dreal|  +  |dreal − dsynth| (Eq. 24)
      model error            GT noise η

The aerial prior is smooth by construction, so η is largest at object boundaries and surface discontinuities — precisely the pixels where the model correctly assigns low reliability. When η dominates the model-error term, low-reliability pixels exhibit high measured error even where the prediction itself is accurate, and the rank correlation inverts.

Two observations rule out the alternative explanation. The inversion shrinks monotonically as the model fits the prior better; overconfidence would deepen it. And the same head, trained identically, yields +0.371 on NYU and +0.380 on KITTI.

Note also that η does not come from the additive per-pixel noise term, which is spatially white. It arises because the prior's base, lateral and object components are smooth by construction and therefore deviate most from real geometry exactly at discontinuities.

Methodological consequence

Reliability metrics are interpretable only when the ground-truth noise floor is uncorrelated with the model's own uncertainty.

Synthetic depth rarely guarantees that. We therefore treat every aerial RADI value in this paper as provisional, and all reliability conclusions rest on KITTI and NYU alone.

This bound applies to our own benchmarks first. It also applies to any confidence-aware depth evaluation performed on simulated data.

Proxy validation against DublinCity aerial LiDAR over 30 frames: MAE 1.4 m, about 7% of mean depth, Pearson r = 0.81. Structurally reasonable at the scale of ground gradient, terrain undulation and building footprints; poor for specular surfaces, sensor-specific artefacts, camera roll beyond 60° and moving objects.

§9 · §10

What generalises, what does not, and what we would not claim.

Transfers unchanged

The components that hold across domains address sparsity and scale rather than appearance: log normalisation, the sparse-anchor term and SILog, which together let one set of loss weights cover an 8× range in depth.

Domain-dependent

The appearance-driven components. Cross-modal attention contributes most where RGB carries usable texture and least on the aerial prior, whose surfaces are smooth by construction. Deformable convolution matters more as anchor density falls, which is why the 0.1% indoor pattern benefits more than the 5% KITTI pattern. A practitioner porting R³DC to a new sensor should expect the losses to transfer and the fusion hyperparameters to need attention.

Order of operations for a new deployment

Fix dmin, dmax and anchor density first — they determine log normalisation and dominate everything else. Keep the loss weights. Tune Nmax against the memory budget before touching anything else in the encoder. And validate reliability with RADI on real ground truth from the target sensor before letting any downstream module consume R̂. That last point is not a formality.

Threats to validity

  • BaselinesCSPN++, NLSPN and CompletionFormer were retrained by us on NYU and on the aerial distribution using published recipes without per-dataset hyperparameter search. Read them as competent rather than optimal.
  • SeedsApart from the multi-seed study and the ensemble baseline, results come from one run per configuration. Differences of 0.001 m — notably T = 6 against T = 9 — sit within plausible seed variance and support no conclusion. The gate-substitution differences are an order of magnitude larger.
  • Offline masksRADI's region masks are derived from ground truth, which makes it a diagnostic rather than an online monitor. Online regional analysis would need masks derived from observable inputs.
  • Generator biasThe aerial benchmarks measure fit to a generator we designed, which favours smooth solutions and therefore plausibly favours our model.

Limitations we name

Raw KITTI accuracy is not state of the art — GuideFormer and BP-Net are ahead by roughly 100–160 mm. Reliability is unsupervised, which slows calibration: REC took 18 epochs to move from −0.508 to −0.149 on VisDrone. It is estimated per frame with no temporal consistency. And the indoor variant is parameter-efficient to train but still requires the 94.6 M-parameter frozen backbone at inference, so its efficiency should not be read as end-to-end deployment efficiency.

The most promising extension is a direct correctness loss. The gating pathway only supervises reliability where refinement can change the output, so in large homogeneous areas far from any anchor the gradient reaching R̂ is weak, and calibration there is inherited from the shared-feature coupling with σ̂ rather than learned.
